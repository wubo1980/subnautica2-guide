Subnautica 2 May 2026 Updates: 4 Million Sold & What's New

Subnautica 2's early access launched May 14 and sold 4 million copies in its first week. Two major patches already landed, and Unknown Worlds has shared their post-launch roadmap. Here's everything you need to know.

🚀 4 Million Copies Sold in Early Access Week

Unknown Worlds Entertainment announced that Subnautica 2 sold over 4 million copies within its first week of Early Access on Steam and Xbox Game Preview. The sequel to the beloved underwater survival game launched on May 14, 2026, and quickly became one of the fastest-selling Early Access titles of the year.

4,000,000+
Copies Sold in Week 1

This puts Subnautica 2 well ahead of the original Subnautica's launch trajectory. The original game sold around 1.2 million copies in its first month of Early Access back in 2018, and Subnautica 2 has more than tripled that pace. The strong launch reflects years of pent-up demand for a proper sequel, as well as the game's striking new visuals and expanded scope.

Player reviews on Steam are "Very Positive" (88% positive as of late May). Common praise includes the revamped crafting system, the new biome variety (now 9 biomes at launch vs the original's 4), and the smoother onboarding. The most common criticism points to occasional performance hitches on older hardware and a few progression bugs that subsequent patches have started addressing.

🔧 May 22 Update — New Resource Zone & Fish AI

The first major content patch dropped on May 22, just over a week after launch. This was the first planned update in Unknown Worlds' early access cycle, and it delivered meaningful additions beyond bug fixes.

New Resource Zone: The Abyssal Fissure

The May 22 update introduces a new explorable area: the Abyssal Fissure, a deep trench biome located southwest of the starting Lifepod. It's accessible by descending through a narrow crevasse at roughly 300 meters depth. This zone is rich in rare materials:

  • Uraninite Crystals — a new resource used in advanced reactor components
  • Thermal Vents — natural heat sources that can power base modules without thermal generators
  • Bioluminescent Flora — unique plants for crafting aesthetic base decorations

The Abyssal Fissure also introduces a new passive creature — the Lantern Ray — which emits a soft glow and swims in schools near the thermal vents. Scanning it unlocks uncraftable decorative items in the Fabricator's new Cosmetic tab.

Fish AI Improvements

One of the update's biggest quality-of-life changes is the overhauled fish AI. Previously, many fish — especially smaller species like Peepers and Boomerang Fish — had erratic, physics-driven swimming patterns that made them frustrating to catch. The new AI introduces realistic schooling behavior:

  • Fish now travel in cohesive groups with coordinated movement
  • Schooling fish scatter when threatened (by you or a predator), then regroup
  • Predators like Stalkers and Sand Sharks now stalk prey with more natural hunting patterns
  • Fish react more realistically to the player's proximity and movement speed

The result is a living ocean that feels more immersive. Catching food is also easier — schools of Peepers now follow predictable routes, making them less frustrating to hunt.

Performance Optimizations

The update brought significant engine-side improvements targeting the most common sources of stuttering and frame drops:

  • Terrain streaming: Reduced pop-in by 40% on all platforms through improved LOD streaming
  • Water rendering: Optimized the volumetric water shader, saving ~8ms per frame on mid-range GPUs
  • Base rendering: Improved multi-room base lighting calculations, reducing CPU overhead when building large structures
  • Memory management: Fixed a memory leak in the resource node spawning system that caused progressively worse performance on long play sessions
📊 Performance Note

Players on PC with NVIDIA RTX 3060 or better now see stable 60 FPS at 1440p High settings. The original launch had frequent dips to 40 FPS in base-building areas. The May 22 patch closes most of that gap.

🩹 May 26 Hotfix 2

Four days after the May 22 update, Unknown Worlds pushed Hotfix 2 to address issues that slipped through. This was a targeted bugfix patch with no new content.

✓
Fabricator craft queue: Fixed a crash when rapidly queuing multiple items while inventory is full. This was the most-reported crash since launch.
✓
Abyssal Fissure geometry: Fixed an exploit where players could clip through walls near the thermal vents using the Prawn Suit's jump jet. Also fixed a soft-lock spot where a player could fall through the world.
✓
Scanner room HUD: Fixed a bug where the Scanner Room's camera drone feeds would corrupt the on-screen UI after repeated uses, leaving persistent visual artifacts.
✓
Gamepad vibration: Fixed controller vibration not triggering on creature attacks in certain biomes (Kelp Forest, Grassy Plateau).
✓
Xbox save corruption: Fixed a rare issue where save files on Xbox Series X/S would become unreadable after the May 22 update. Affected saves are restored upon relaunch.
✓
Multiplayer desync: Fixed a desynchronization issue when two players enter a Cyclops submarine simultaneously on the host's instance.

🗺️ Post-Launch Roadmap

Unknown Worlds shared their planned Early Access update cadence on the Subnautica 2 Steam Community page. The roadmap is structured around quarterly content drops, with smaller hotfix patches in between.

Phase 1 - May to July 2026

New Biome: The Crystal Caverns

A crystalline cave system at extreme depths (500-800m). Features new resources (Resonant Quartz, Crystal Shards) and a new predatory leviathan-class creature. Alongside: additional base pieces, new fish species, and quality-of-life improvements to the crafting menu.

Phase 2 - August to October 2026

Vehicles Expansion

The first major vehicle content update. Includes the return of the Seamoth (with new modules like a sonar upgrade and a torpedo bay), a brand-new deep-sea submersible called the "Nautilus" designed for extreme pressure environments, and enhanced Cyclops customization options (paint, interior modules).

Phase 3 - November 2026 to January 2027

Story Content & Surface Biome

The first major story expansion. Adds a new story arc with voice-acted radio transmissions, data logs, and a surface-access area (floating islands above the ocean). The surface biome introduces above-water base building and new exploration challenges tied to weather cycles.

Phase 4 - February to April 2027

Deep Sea Ecosystem Expansion

Expands the deepest regions of the map with a new "Hadal Zone" biome at over 1200 meters. Introduces pressure-resistant base building, exotic deep-sea creatures, and a new endgame crafting tier. This phase is expected to be the largest content update of the Early Access cycle.

1.0 Launch - Mid 2027 (Target)

Full Release

Targeting a 1.0 release in mid-2027 with all planned content, final balance tuning, full controller support, Steam Deck verification, and console optimization passes. The exact date depends on player feedback and development progress.

What This Means for Players

If you've been on the fence about Subnautica 2, now is a great time to jump in. The day-one experience is solid — the early game is polished, the beginner's crafting loop is well-designed, and the first content patch has already addressed the most common complaints. With 4 million players already in the water, the community is active and the developers are clearly committed to the Early Access model.

For existing players, the Abyssal Fissure gives you a reason to dive deeper, and the performance improvements make exploration smoother than at launch. The roadmap promises substantial content through 2026 and into 2027, with vehicles and story content arriving before the end of the year.

🔥 Bottom Line

4 million sales speak for themselves. Subnautica 2 is off to an incredible start, the patches are coming fast, and the roadmap is ambitious. Whether you're a new player or a returning fan, the deep ocean has never looked better.
