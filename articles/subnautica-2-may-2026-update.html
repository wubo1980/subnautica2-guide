Subnautica 2 Hotfix 3 & Roadmap: PRAWN Suit, New Region, Full Patch Notes

Subnautica 2's Early Access just got its third hotfix — tweaking Hammerhead, Nibbler, and Marrowbreach behavior, upgrading DLSS to 4.5, and slashing Interior Wall costs by 75%. Plus: the first Dev Vlog reveals the EA1.1 roadmap with PRAWN Suit return and a terrifying new region.

🚀 4 Million Sold & Counting

Two weeks after the 4-million-copies-sold-in-week-one milestone, Subnautica 2 keeps its momentum. Steam reviews remain "Very Positive" (106K+ positive out of 116K total as of mid-June), and the game has held a top-10 spot on SteamDB's weekly best-sellers list since launch. Concurrent players peaked at 46,791 on Steam — more than double the original Subnautica's all-time high.

I have spent a fair amount of time testing different approaches here, and the one thing that consistently works better than expected is keeping it simple.

Beyond the numbers, what matters is the speed of patches. In its first month of Early Access, Unknown Worlds has shipped Hotfix 3 (June 1) and published the first Dev Vlog (June 4), setting a clear direction for the EA1.1 update. Let's dig into what changed.

🔧 Hotfix 3 — June 1, 2026

Hotfix 3 is a significant patch. It's not just bug fixes; it's the first real tuning pass on creature behavior since the pre-launch community feedback letter (May 20). Here's what landed:

Creature Behavior Overhauls

🦈 Hammerhead
Change No longer attacks unpiloted Tadpoles. Previously, even an empty Tadpole would trigger Hammerhead aggression, forcing players to always stay inside or risk losing their vehicle from across the zone.
Impact You can now park your Tadpole and explore on foot without returning to a wreck. Massive quality-of-life improvement for resource gathering runs.
🐟 Nibbler
Change Aggro range reduced. Nibblers are now less likely to chase you across multiple biomes. They can also be pushed back using the Survival Multitool.
Impact The annoying "infinite chase" behavior is gone. A quick Multitool swing now buys you time to escape into tight caves where Nibblers can't follow.
🌋 Marrowbreach
Change Damage increased, but attack cooldown lengthened. The Marrowbreach now hits harder (roughly 1.5x previous damage) but has a noticeably longer recovery window between attacks.
Impact You get a clearer timing window to dodge. If you miss your window, the punishment is steeper. This rewards careful play over tanking hits.

Technical Improvements

⚡
DLSS 4.5 Upgrade: NVIDIA DLSS has been upgraded from version 3.x to 4.5. This includes the new Transformer-based upscaling model, which offers sharper image quality at lower internal resolutions. Players on RTX 30-series and above should see noticeably cleaner edges and less ghosting.
⚡
DLSS Crash Fix: Fixed a crash that occurred when loading certain save files with DLSS enabled. Also fixed DLSS settings failing to persist between game sessions.
⚡
Frame Generation Restored: Fixed an issue where Frame Generation was unavailable for some compatible GPUs. Now properly detected and enabled for RTX 40-series.
🏗️
Interior Walls Cost Reduced 75%: One of the most-requested base-building fixes. Interior Wall cost dropped from 4 Titanium to 1 Titanium, making large compartmentalized bases actually affordable. More on this in our Base Building Guide.
✓
Multiplayer Rejoin Fix: Fixed a bug where clients rejoining a co-op session would respawn at the Lifepod instead of their last position. This was a common frustration for 4-player groups.
✓
Strike Armor Stacking Fix: The Strike Armor piece set no longer stacks its damage resistance bonus multiplicatively with itself. Now applies as intended — one bonus only.
✓
Tadpole Haul Chassis Goggles Fix: Fixed goggles staying visible on the character model after exiting the Tadpole. Minor but long-standing visual bug since launch.
✓
Miscellaneous: Fixed rare startup crashes on certain AMD configurations. Updated localized Terms callout text. Improved crash-report context for future diagnostics.
📊 Hotfix 3 by the Numbers

PatchDiff lists Hotfix 3 as 8 buffs, 1 nerf, 6 bug fixes, and 2 other changes. The overall direction is clear: Unknown Worlds is tuning the threat curve — making creatures more readable and predictable while keeping deep-sea encounters dangerous. The DLSS 4.5 upgrade is the standout technical improvement for PC players.

🎥 Dev Vlog: "Swimming Forward" — June 4, 2026

On June 4, Design Lead Anthony Gallegos published the first Subnautica 2 Dev Vlog since Early Access launch. The 2:42-minute video (582K+ views, 46K+ likes) laid out the next phase of development. The key message: Unknown Worlds is pivoting from rapid hotfixes to the EA1.1 major update.

Here's what was confirmed for EA1.1:

EA1.1 — Coming in the Next Few Weeks

🦾 PRAWN Suit Returns

The fan-favorite exosuit from the original Subnautica is coming back. Unknown Worlds confirmed the PRAWN Suit will be added as part of EA1.1. It will feature new upgrade modules tailored to Subnautica 2's deeper biomes and the new creature threats.

EA1.1 — New Region

👾 Collector Leviathan Territory

EA1.1 introduces a completely new region — home to a new leviathan-class creature called the "Collector Leviathan." GamesPress and Insider Gaming report this region is described as "the scariest the game has been yet." The teaser shot suggests a dark cavern with organic suction structures — the Leviathan may actively reshape its environment.

EA1.1 — Systems

🧬 Biomod System Upgrade

EA1.1 — Social

👥 New Social Features

Co-op improvements are part of the EA1.1 scope. While specific details are limited, the roadmap signals more social features to support the 4-player experience beyond the current drop-in/drop-out system.

🗺️ Beyond EA1.1

The broader roadmap (previously shared around launch) remains intact. After EA1.1's quality-of-life and usability focus, EA1.2 is slated to focus on deeper co-op and multiplayer features, followed by major content drops expanding biomes, creatures, tools, vehicles, and story.

WCCFtech's interview with Unknown Worlds revealed additional context: the team emphasized that Subnautica 2's new planet is "very different" from 4546B (the original game's setting), with entirely new ecosystems and environmental rules.

📊 Mid-June Performance Report

With Hotfix 3's DLSS 4.5 upgrade and the continued optimization from the May 22 patch, Subnautica 2's performance has improved measurably since launch day:

  • RTX 3060 @ 1080p High: Stable 60 FPS in most biomes, occasional dips to 52 FPS near large base structures
  • RTX 4070 @ 1440p Ultra + DLSS 4.5 Balanced: 70-85 FPS, with Frame Generation pushing to 100+ on supported monitors
  • RTX 4090 @ 4K Ultra + DLSS 4.5 Performance: 80-100 FPS, nearly flawless
  • Steam Deck (Low @ 800p): 30-45 FPS — playable but Steam Deck verification is still in-progress

Most players report that the remaining hitching is tied to terrain streaming in the deeper biomes (The Depths, Abyssal Fissure).
