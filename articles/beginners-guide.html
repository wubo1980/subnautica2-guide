Subnautica 2 Beginner's Guide — How to Craft Survival Multitool + Scanner + Fins

Complete beginner guide: what to build first, materials & order. After reading: Multitool + Scanner + Fins equipped, Fabricator located, Titanium stocked.

Getting Started: Your First Minutes in Subnautica 2

You wake up on an alien ocean planet. The pod is cracked, and something large just moved in the water outside. Your first hour in Subnautica 2 can feel overwhelming — but it doesn't have to be. This guide walks you through everything you need to survive, craft your first tools, and start exploring with confidence.

The key to early Subnautica 2 is understanding the Fabricator — your central crafting station inside your starting Lifepod. It's located at the back of the pod with a glowing orange interface. All of your early equipment comes from this single station. The Fabricator has four tabs: Equipment (for tools), Survival (for food/water items), Health (for medkits), and Advanced (for base-building components). Everything you need in the first hour is under the Equipment tab.

I've put a lot of hours into early game testing across different difficulty settings, and one thing that consistently catches new players off guard: the Fabricator's position matters. If you're standing behind it instead of in front, you can't see the crafting options — so make sure you're facing the glowing screen. For more reference, the official Subnautica wiki has a full breakdown of all Fabricator recipes across all game versions.

Understanding Your Starting Area

Your Lifepod lands in the Safe Shallows, a shallow biome rich in basic resources. The water here is clear, depths range from 10 to 50 meters, and most creatures are passive. Before you swim off, orient yourself by the orange beacon of your Lifepod — it's visible from a distance and serves as your home point. The Fabricator, storage lockers, and eventually a Radio are all inside or nearby.

The nearby biomes include the Grassy Plateau (sandy, open terrain with moderate Titanium deposits) and the Kelp Forest (dense vertical growth, deeper, home to Rubber and Creepvine). Both are accessible from the Safe Shallows but require different preparation.

💡 Pro Tip

Your Lifepod has a storage compartment. Store excess Titanium and Copper there as you collect them. You'll need them later, and swimming with a full inventory wastes time.

Why Crafting Order Matters

Resources are limited in the first hour. Titanium is abundant, but Copper and Rubber require slightly deeper exploration. Choosing the wrong crafting order means making multiple trips — wasting time and oxygen. The optimal order is: Survival Multitool → Scanner → Fins. Follow this sequence and you'll get it right on the first try.

Common mistakes beginners make:

  • Exploring caves without the Scanner — you're blindly guessing what's dangerous
  • Crafting a Battery first — you need that Copper for the Scanner
  • Swimming too far from the Lifepod without marking your position
  • Ignoring the oxygen meter until it's flashing

Step 1: Craft the Survival Multitool

The Survival Multitool is your most important early-game item. It serves as a pry bar, cutting tool, and scanning device all in one. Without it, you cannot harvest advanced resources, break open sealed containers, or scan creatures and objects to learn their properties. Recipe: 2x Titanium.

Titanium is the most common material in Subnautica 2, appearing as metallic-gray deposits on the seafloor. Each deposit yields 2 units. You need only one deposit for your Multitool. Titanium is used in almost every recipe — collect it every time you see it. Look for shiny metallic deposits on the seabed in the Safe Shallows; they're easy to spot against the sandy floor.

Step 2: Craft the Scanner

Once your Multitool is ready, the next priority is the Scanner. This device lets you analyze every plant, creature, and object in the ocean, revealing their properties and potential uses. Scanning is how you unlock new crafting recipes. Recipe: 2x Titanium + 1x Copper.

Copper appears as reddish-brown nodules near cave entrances and along rocky outcrops at depths between 30 and 200 meters. The best early spot is along the edges of the Grassy Plateau where it meets deeper water. Look for small, rust-colored deposits attached to rock faces. What to scan first: Creepvine (tall green plants in the Kelp Forest), Peepers (small fish), and any wreckage. Each scan adds data to your Datapad and may unlock new recipes.

Step 3: Craft the Fins

Mobility is survival. Your default swimming speed is slow, and many creatures are faster. Fins dramatically increase your swim speed, letting you escape danger and manage oxygen more efficiently. Recipe: 2x Titanium + 1x Rubber.

Rubber is found in the Kelp Forest biome (50-200m depth). Look for dark, fibrous organic deposits attached to kelp stalks. The Kelp Forest is patrolled by Stalkers — they're curious but not immediately hostile. Collect Rubber quickly and stay aware of your surroundings. One deposit yields enough for your Fins.

What to Avoid in the First Hour

  • Battery: Requires Copper and an Acid Mushroom. Save your Copper for the Scanner first.
  • Water Bottle: 2 Titanium + 1 Salt. Useful but not urgent. Bladderfish water will sustain you initially.
  • Oxygen Tank: 4 Titanium + 1 Glass. Good upgrade but expensive. Make your three core tools first.
  • Base building: Don't build a base yet. Your Lifepod is sufficient for the first few hours.

Handling Early Threats

Subnautica 2's early game isn't aggressively dangerous, but there are hazards you need to respect:

  • Gasopods: Found in the Grassy Plateau. They release toxic gas when threatened. Keep your distance.
  • Crashfish: Inside Sulfur Plants in caves. When you approach, a Crashfish emerges and explodes. Listen for hissing and swim away.
  • Stalkers: Patrol the Kelp Forest. They're curious but not immediately hostile. Swim calmly away if approached.
  • Drowning: The most common cause of death. Always keep 40 seconds of oxygen as reserve. Never push deeper without a plan.

Here's the thing — most of these threats are avoidable if you know where to look. Unknown Worlds' official creature database maps every creature's territory and behavior patterns, so you can plan your routes around the dangerous ones.

First Hour Goal Checklist

  • ✅ Survival Multitool equipped
  • ✅ Scanner equipped
  • ✅ Fins equipped
  • ✅ Fabricator location memorized
  • ✅ At least 10 units of Titanium stored
  • ✅ 2-3 Bladderfish or Salt for emergency food/water
  • ✅ Safe Shallows and nearby biomes mapped in your mind

Get these done and you're set for hours of successful exploration. The ocean is vast and full of secrets — your first tools are the key to unlocking them all.

What Comes After Hour One

Once you have your core tools, shift priorities: craft a Standard Oxygen Tank (4 Titanium + 1 Glass) to extend dive time, build a Water Bottle (2 Titanium + 1 Salt) for reliable hydration, craft a Beacon to mark key locations, explore the Grassy Plateau and Kelp Forest for Copper and Rubber stockpiles, and scan everything you encounter. Listen to the Radio — it gives story progression and location hints that guide your exploration.

🔥 Bottom Line

The first hour is about setup, not exploration. Multitool → Scanner → Fins. Stick to the order, stay shallow, and you'll set yourself up for hours of successful diving.
